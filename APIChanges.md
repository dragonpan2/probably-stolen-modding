# Probably Stolen: Modding API Changes

中文版本：[APIChanges.zh-CN.md](APIChanges.zh-CN.md)

What changed in the modding API, per game update, newest on top. Within each update, entries are ordered by importance: things you must act on first, new capabilities last. Full documentation lives in [ModdingGuide.md](ModdingGuide.md).

## November 4, 2026

**New**

- **A Steam auth session ticket for your own server, issued by the game: `ModHelper.RequestSteamAuthTicket(serverIdentity, onTicket)` and `CancelSteamAuthTicket()`, under the new `steam-auth` capability.** A mod with a server (multiplayer, leaderboards) can now prove to it that the player is a real Steam client on an account that owns the game, which is what keeps pirated and emulated copies out: the server validates the bytes with `ISteamGameServer::BeginAuthSession` (a headless game server under the game's App ID, anonymous logon, no key from anyone). The identity is the one your server gave you, `steamid:<id>` or `ip:<address>:<port>`, and the ticket is bound to it. One ticket per mod, dropped by a new request, when the mod is stopped and at quit. The player sees the capability as "Prove that you own the game to this mod's online service", in amber; it is not part of `steam` and `steam` is not part of it. Calling `SteamUser.GetAuthSessionTicket` yourself stays refused. The scanner counts a call to these two methods as `steam-auth`, and the game checks the manifest again when the call runs: without the declaration it returns `false` and logs why. `ModSteamAuth`, the class behind it, joins the guard types: a Harmony patch or reflection aimed at it refuses the activation. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#proving-the-players-steam-identity-to-your-server)
- **Smaller additions.** `ModCapability.SteamAuth` (`"steam-auth"`); `ModCapability.Risk.High` is now in use, for it.

## November 3, 2026

**New**

- **`StoreClient.onShotSurvived`** runs the moment a shot wounds a `survivesShot` client (`StoreUIManager.ShootSurvivor`), before the wound frames and whether or not `shotSurvivedDialogue` gets to play: the place to record that the character was shot. Vanilla uses it on Lou.
- **`PlayerStore.CloseWantedPoster(string posterId)`** closes a wanted poster by id (status 1, no reward, no `onCaptureAction`), for a capture that happens without the character at the counter. The `StoreClient` overload is unchanged.

## November 2, 2026

- **The shotgun on story clients: four `StoreClient` hooks.** `holsterResumesDialogue` makes drawing the gun PAUSE the client's conversation instead of dropping it (`DialogUIManager.PauseConversation`), and holstering resume it where it was cut (`ResumeConversation`): the client stays instead of being dismissed. `unholsterDialogue` is the client's own reaction to the gun (null: the random pool, `AugHelper.GetClientReactionUnholster`). `shotCountsAsCapture` makes Security treat a shot wanted client without augs as a capture (`AugHelper.SHOT_WANTED_CAPTURE`, third result of `GetShootResult`): the bounty, the Security reputation, the faction change, the slip and `onCaptureAction`, through `PlayerStore.OnWantedClientShot`; `shotCaptureDialogue` is the officer's monologue after the check (SEC lines; null: `StoreClientDialogList.SecShotWantedGenericDialog`). `survivesShot` makes the shot wound instead of kill: the flash and the wound frames play, nobody falls and Security is not called; `shotSurvivedDialogue` plays once the gun is back in the rack and must end by dismissing the client (`StoreClientMono.SetLimpOnLeave` slows the walk out). `forceShootable` lets the gun come out on a client who is not dismissable or not arrestable. `onShotDead` runs after the standard manslaughter penalties (`AugHelper.CleanupKill`). Vanilla uses them on Serpico, Li Bei Wen, the Butcher and Sawyer.
- **A wanted poster character shot dead as manslaughter now closes their poster** (`PlayerStore.CloseWantedPoster`: `wantedStatus` 1, no reward, no `onCaptureAction`), so their returns stop like after an arrest. Stealth posters are not touched. `PlayerStore.GrantWantedCaptureReward(client, isShot)` and `ApplyReportFactionChange(client)` are the report's reward and faction change, now callable on their own.
**New**

- **Configure, a context menu action for items with a settings window.** Tag the item `GeneralHelper.CONFIGURE_TAG` and set `item.onConfigureSlotItemFunc`; the right-click menu then lists **Configure** on that item only (greyed until the item is the player's) and calls the hook. Activate and Toggle keep their meaning, so an item whose Activate does work can still offer settings. The wrappers are `GameItem.MayConfigureSlotItem`, `CanConfigureSlotItem` and `ConfigureSlotItem`. The saved item state replaces the factory's tags on load, so stamp the tag from `onSlotAddItemFunc` as well (the Outlet Hopper in `HopperHelper.CreateOutlet` is the reference).
- **The right click and the wheel click are key binds.** `KeyBindHelper.SECONDARY_CLICK` (default `Mouse1`) opens an item's context menu, flips or rotates the held item and leaves equip mode, pin mode, a container pick, the storage coordinator's key capture and the magnifying zoom; `KeyBindHelper.FLIP_ITEM_ALT` (default `Mouse2`) flips the held item. `ItemContextHandler.triggerKey` reads the binding and is no longer an inspector field. A mod that reacts to the right click should read `KeyBindHelper.GetKeyDown(KeyBindHelper.SECONDARY_CLICK)` instead of `Input.GetMouseButtonDown(1)`.
- **Mouse buttons can be captured for a key bind**, the left one excepted (`KeyBindHelper.IsReserved(KeyCode.Mouse0)`), for your bindings too. `KeyBindHelper.KeyName` names them "Right Click", "Wheel Click", "Mouse 4" and up. While a capture runs, mouse presses no longer reach the `InputActionManager` handlers (they were the only input that still did).

## November 1, 2026

**Action recommended**

- **Destroying an item now destroys its content window and whatever is still inside it.** `GameItem.Destroy()` on a container (anything with a `contentWindow`) destroys the window's render tree and the items in its inventories, and `item.contentWindow` is null afterwards. Before, the window stayed behind hidden for the whole session and kept the dead item and its contents in memory. A mod that destroyed a container and then read its contents through the old window must move them out first (`GraphUtils.TryAcceptAll` or `GameItem.TryUnloadAll`).

**New**

- **`PixelWindow.Destroy()`** releases a window you created yourself (its render tree, dock, raised canvas and root registrations). Windows set with `SetContentWindow` need nothing: the item takes them along.
- **`GraphUtils.EmporiumTryAdd(node, item)` now routes overflow through Intake Hoppers.** When the node refuses the item, the Intake Hoppers sitting in that inventory get it before the store chain, in their night order, each under its own filter (the keep count does not apply); the outlet's rules decide through the normal accept path, a partly taken stack goes on to the next intake, whatever nobody takes spills as before. This is `HopperHelper.TryRouteOverflow(GameInventory grid, GameItem item)`, public for machines that place output some other way. Vanilla machines that used to destroy or lose output that no longer fit (Meat Culture, the Furnace's second ingot, the Printer, the Desequencer) now spill it through `EmporiumTryAdd` like the planter; a modded machine should place its output the same way so hoppers carry it.
- **`StallWatchdog.Crumb(text)`** leaves a breadcrumb for the stall report. When the main thread finishes no frame for five seconds, the game appends the last 24 breadcrumbs with the memory counters to its `GameLog_*.txt` and logs the duration once it resumes. Call it from any thread at the start of work that can take long, one short line.

## October 31, 2026 (security update 2)

**Action recommended**

- **Harmony patches or reflection aimed at `PaperBookRegistry` refuse the activation.** Calling its public methods and the `ModHelper` paper functions works as before. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#hard-refusals)
- **Book files are read and written only inside the mod folders.** `PaperBookRegistry.Save`, `LoadFile` and `ReloadFromDisk` refuse a path outside `Mods/` and your mod's own folder, or one that is not a `.json` file, and log an error instead.
- **Another mod's data folder is read-only.** `ReadModDataFileText` / `Bytes`, `ModDataFileExists` and `ListModDataFiles` still work with any mod's id, but `WriteModDataFileText` / `Bytes` and `DeleteModDataFile` throw `UnauthorizedAccessException` when the id names the data folder of another installed mod. Your own id in any spelling that maps to your folder (`"coolmod"` for `CoolMod`) and ids no installed mod uses keep working. A write that no mod's code on the call stack explains (code a Harmony transpiler inserted into a game method, or emitted code) is refused for every installed mod's folder, your own included: call these from your own methods. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#reading-and-writing-files-without-the-capability)
- **Approved mods are scanned again once** at the first start of this version.

**New**

- **Asset bundles without the `files` capability.** `ModHelper.LoadModAssetBundle(modId, relativePath)` loads a bundle from your mod folder, and only the `AssetBundle` loaders that take a path (`LoadFromFile`, `LoadFromFileAsync`, `RecompressAssetBundleAsync`) still count as `files`: `LoadFromMemory`, `LoadAsset` and the rest are free. A mod that declared `files` only for its bundle can drop it. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#reading-and-writing-files-without-the-capability)
- **`DifficultyRules.louDemandValue`**: the Credits of goods Lou demands after her disguise visit (cozy 500, normal 750, hard 1000, challenge 1500). A difficulty registered with `AddDifficulty` copies it from its `basedOn`. The removed constant `StoreClientData.LOU_DEMAND_VALUE` is now `StoreClientData.GetLouDemandValue()`. The bomb threshold of her collection visit is half that value, `StoreClientData.GetLouDemandMinimum()`; the constant `LOU_DEMAND_MINIMUM` is `[Obsolete]` and no longer read.
- **Smaller additions.** `ModLoader.CallingModId()` returns the id of the nearest mod on the current call stack, or null for the game's own code (what the data folder rule above uses). `ModWorkshop.TryGetStats(publishedFileId, out ItemStats)` gives a Workshop item's first upload date and up and down votes once Steam has answered (`ItemStats.createdUtc`, `votesUp`, `votesDown`, `Ratings`). `ModCapability.Risk` and `RiskOf(capability)` give the risk level the activation dialog colours each capability with (`Severe` for native, dynamic code, files, external reflection and network; `Medium` for steam and process).

## October 30, 2026 (security update)

**Action recommended**

- **Starting programs is never allowed.** `System.Diagnostics.Process` / `ProcessStartInfo` refuse the activation whatever the manifest says, and the game blocks `Process.Start` from mod code at runtime (the mod is stopped and deactivated). The `process` capability now covers only `Application.OpenURL` with a literal `http://` or `https://` address, and the player sees it as "Open web links in your browser". [Details](ModdingGuide.md#hard-refusals)
- **Steam API use is the new `steam` capability, and some Steam calls are never allowed.** Achievements, friends, overlay, Workshop queries and other Steamworks calls must be declared as `<Capability>steam</Capability>`. Creating, updating, deleting, subscribing or downloading Workshop items, Steam Cloud writes, authentication tickets, chat messages and invites, `SteamAPI` / `SteamClient`, and a `DllImport` into Steam refuse the activation whatever the manifest says, and the game blocks those calls at runtime: a mod caught making one is stopped and deactivated. Publish with steamcmd as before. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#hard-refusals)
- **Your whole mod folder is scanned, not only the DLLs.** Executables and scripts under any name, and files of unknown format that look encrypted or compressed, refuse the activation; images, sounds, text, fonts and PDBs are fine. Remove zips, installers and source art from what you publish. There is no size limit. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#capabilities-and-the-scanner)
- **Pass your own id to `Harmony.Unpatch`.** `Unpatch(original, type)` defaults to the id `"*"` (every owner) and is now refused; write `harmony.Unpatch(original, type, harmony.Id)`.
- **Approved mods are scanned again once** at the first start of this version, so a mod that relied on one of these calls stops loading until its author updates it.

## October 2026 (1.0 release)

**Action recommended**

- **`manifest.xml` needs `<Assemblies>` and `<Capabilities>`.** A manifest without both is not listed. List every DLL to load under `<Assemblies>` (any other DLL in the folder refuses the activation, as does a DLL named like a game or framework assembly), and declare under `<Capabilities>` what your code does beyond the game's API: `files`, `network`, `process`, `dynamic-code`, `native`, `reflection-external`. The element is usually empty. When a player activates your mod the game scans your DLLs: an undeclared capability refuses the activation, declared ones are shown to the player before they approve. Check yours with the console command `mod-scan <id>`. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#capabilities-and-the-scanner)
- **Approval is per computer and per version.** The player's Activate click records the hashes of your files. An update that adds no capability keeps running; one that adds a capability waits until the player reviews it again. While you iterate on a local mod, turn on **Developer mode** in the Mods menu so your rebuilds are trusted. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#what-players-see)
- **The F8 console and F9 dev menu need `dev.txt`.** Both are off in the shipped game until an empty file named `dev.txt` exists next to the game's executable. Create it once on your development machine; nothing changes for your mod. The Marketing Panel is now developer-only, so buttons registered under `DEBUG_CATEGORY_MARKETING` land in Special. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#dev-tools)
- **Every mod needs a DLL with an `IMod` class.** A folder with only assets (no DLL, or no DLL implementing `IMod`) is no longer listed in the Mods menu; the console logs `[Your Mod Name] Error: ...` instead. Ship a minimal `IMod` class with asset-only mods. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#mod-assets)
- **Stop patching `TableMaster` / `TableGroupMaster` for loot changes.** Their static dictionaries are gone; rolls now go through `LootRegistry`, which has a supported API for exactly that. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#loot-tables)

**New**

- **Players see your mod's name.** It is the last line of the tooltip of the items you add, and the conversation recorder shows "From Your Mod Name:" before the first line of the customers you add (both are player options, on by default). Items registered from your DLL and customers added from an `OnGenerateCustomer*` hook are recognized automatically; `ModHelper.SetClientSource(client, modId)` marks a customer added any other way, `ModHelper.SetSource` an item whose factory is not your code. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#adding-items-through-the-item-directory-hook)
- **Your own appraisal items.** `ModHelper.AddAppraisalItem(modId, id, itemId, unlockRank, createRandom, getCorrectValue, isType, guideItemId, displayName)` adds an item type appraisal customers bring once the player's Appraiser Board rank unlocks it (several per rank allowed; the same id replaces a vanilla type). The estimate is graded against your `getCorrectValue`. `RemoveAppraisalItems(modId)` takes them off, `GetAppraiserRank()` reads the rank. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#appraisal-items)
- **Files without the `files` capability.** `ModHelper.ReadModFileText / ReadModFileBytes / ModFileExists / GetModFolder` read from your mod folder; `GetModDataFolder`, `ReadModDataFileText / Bytes`, `WriteModDataFileText / Bytes`, `DeleteModDataFile`, `ListModDataFiles` read and write a per-mod folder in the save location. Relative paths only. Using these instead of `System.IO` means nothing to declare. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#reading-and-writing-files-without-the-capability)
- **Console commands** `mod-scan <id or folder>` (what the scanner finds in your DLLs and the version's fingerprint hash), `mod-trust [id]` (the approvals on this computer) and `mod-trust-list` (the remote list state). [Details](ModdingGuide.md#checking-before-you-publish)
- **Difficulties are data.** Four difficulties now (cozy, normal, hard, challenge) and `ModHelper.AddDifficulty(modId, id, order, title, subtitle, configure, basedOn, notes, iconKey)` adds yours: a set of `DifficultyRules` values the game reads directly, listed on the selection screen by order and described from the rules that differ from Normal. `ModifyDifficulty(id, modify)` tweaks any difficulty, `RemoveDifficulties(modId)` takes yours off, `GetDifficultyId()` / `GetDifficultyRules()` / `IsDifficultyAtLeast(id)` read the current one, and `ModHook.OnDifficultyApplied(id)` fires when a run starts or loads. `PlayerStore.IsHardMode()` now means hard or harder. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#difficulties)
- **Your own starting perks.** `ModHelper.AddStartingPerk(modId, id, cost, type, name, description, iconKey, incompatiblePerks, extraPerkSlots, mindEditable)` lists a perk on the new game perk screen with the vanilla ones; the pick is saved with the run and your mod applies the effect after reading `IsStartingPerkActive(id)` / `GetStartingPerkIds()`. `RemoveStartingPerks(modId)` takes them off. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#starting-perks)
- **Your own starting scenarios.** `ModHelper.AddStartingScenario(modId, id, title, description, startWith, onStart, iconKey, tag, tagTone, skipIntro, comingSoon)` adds a specialization to the new game screen's icon strip; `onStart` places the starting items when the run begins. `RemoveStartingScenarios(modId)` takes them off, `GetStartingScenarioId()` / `IsStartingScenario(id)` tell which scenario a run began with, and `ModHook.OnNewGameStarted(id)` fires for every new run. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#starting-scenarios)
- **Your own Wild's Network upgrades.** `ModHelper.AddNetworkUpgrade(modId, id, cost, subtitle, title, description, onUnlock, tab, iconKey, prerequisites, cooldownDays, ...)` puts an upgrade into the rebuilt Wild's Network window: the card is the subtitle, the tab is any string (empty for the Basic tab, `SetNetworkTabLabel` names it), prerequisites on the same card draw the chain connector, the icon is one of your sprites. State is saved with the run under the id. `RemoveNetworkUpgrades(modId)` takes them off again, `IsNetworkUpgradeUnlocked(id)` and `GetNetworkUpgrade(id)` read state, `OpenWildNetwork()` opens the window, and `ModHook.OnNetworkUpgradeUnlocked(id)` fires for every purchase. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#network-upgrades)
- **Loot table editing.** Add your items to the game's drop tables, change or remove existing drops, and register new tables and groups: `ModHelper.AddLootEntry / SetLootWeight / RemoveLootEntry / RegisterLootTable` and the `Group` equivalents, callable from `OnEnable`. Weights are relative and vanilla tables sum to 1000, so weight `10` is about 1%. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#loot-tables)
- **JSON loot files.** The same edits as data: `LootTables/*.json` in your mod folder, no C# needed. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#editing-tables-with-json-files)
- **`ModHook.OnLootTablesLoaded`** (tables are ready, all edits applied) and **`ModHook.OnLootRolled(LootRollContext)`** (override the result of any roll). [Details](ModdingGuide.md#reacting-to-rolls)
- **Console commands** `loot-list [id]` and `loot-roll <id> [count]` to see effective odds, which mod touched what, and to test-roll a table. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#inspecting-tables-in-the-console)
- **Mod data store.** Save anything not tied to an item with `ModHelper.SetModData / GetModData` (plus `Int`, `Float`, `Bool` variants, `HasModData`, `RemoveModData`, `GetModDataKeys`, `ClearModData`). Namespaced by mod id, saved with the run, restored on load. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#saving-mod-data)
- **Custom UI.** Build your own windows from code with the `CustomUIManager` fluent builder (labels, buttons, toggles, sliders, inputs, dropdowns, progress bars, images, tabs, scroll lists), on any game canvas or a persistent overlay. Mod sprites work through the `"modId:spriteName"` key. Custom UI moves from the "not supported" list to supported. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#custom-ui)
- **Custom UI and machine windows, additions.** The builder gained `AddHeader`, `AddWarning`, `AddSeparator`, `AddCloseButton`, `AddIconButton`, `BeginPanel`, `BeginScrollFlexible` and a third look, `UseMachineryStyle()` / `CustomUIManager.Style.Machinery`: the Machinery content-window style of the machines. Machine windows gained `MachineryRangeSlider`, `MachineryText.WithInfoLink` and `CreateMachineryWindow(canMove, displayOrder, panels)`, which draws a shipped window's panels in another order without touching saves. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#custom-ui)
- **Your own F9 debug buttons.** `ModHelper.AddDebugFunction(modId, category, name, action, tall)` puts a button on the F9 dev menu under Store, Command, Spawn, or Special; `RemoveDebugFunctions(modId)` takes them off again. Callable from `OnEnable`. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#adding-your-own-debug-buttons)
- **Mod configuration.** `ModHelper.GetConfig(modId).Define(...)` gives your mod typed, saved settings (bool, int, float with optional range, string with optional choices, enums) and a generated configuration panel behind the gear button in the Mods menu, with Apply and Reset to Defaults. Values persist per mod in `ModConfigs/<modId>.json`, `ConfigEntry.OnChanged` and `ModHook.OnModConfigApplied` report changes. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#mod-configuration)
- **Steam Workshop (main game only).** Subscribed items load like `Mods/` folders and appear in the Mods menu with a Steam icon, inactive until the player activates them; the menu can unsubscribe and open an item's page. A local folder with the same `<ID>` overrides the Workshop copy. Publishing is done with steamcmd, not in the game. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#steam-workshop)
- **New Mods menu.** Both lists scroll and can be searched by name or id, rows show a warning when a prerequisite is missing or inactive or the game version does not match, and the detail pane lists your `<Prerequisites>` with a check or cross, colours your `<GameVersions>` against the running game, and shows your preview image: the Workshop preview for Workshop items, `preview.png` from your mod folder otherwise (no image, no banner). Auto-Sort orders active mods after their prerequisites.
- **Your settings in the game's Options window.** `ModHelper.AddGameOptionSection / AddGameOptionToggle / AddGameOptionSlider / AddGameOptionDropdown / AddGameOptionKeyBind` add entries (your own section or a built-in one) that the game draws with its widgets, fills from your getter, and writes back through your setter on Apply; `RemoveGameOption / RemoveGameOptions` take them out. Callable from `OnEnable`. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#game-options)
- **Load errors are reported.** When your DLL fails to load or `Init`, `OnEnable`, or a Harmony patch throws, the console logs `[Your Mod Name] Error: <exception>` followed by the full stack trace, and the Mods menu shows a warning icon on your mod, which stays in the active list so it can be deactivated. For mods in the `Mods/` folder the F8 console also opens at launch. `LoadedMod.LoadError` holds the one-line cause. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#troubleshooting)

## July 2026

**Action recommended**

- **Stop playing sounds through your own `AudioSource`.** Mod sounds are now registered with the game's audio system under `<modId>:<key>`. Use `ModHelper.PlaySound / PlaySound2 / PlaySound3(modId, key)` instead, so the player's volume and pause settings apply. Reading `mod.Sounds` directly still works but bypasses volume settings. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#sounds)

**Behavior change**

- **Hook handler exceptions no longer break the game or other mods.** Each subscriber runs in its own try/catch; errors are logged to the F8 console as `[ModHook] ... from '<your mod>' threw: ...` and everything else continues. Throwing from a handler to abort game code no longer works (it was never supported; use a Harmony prefix returning `false`). A logged exception still means your mod is broken.

**New**

- **CSV-based localization.** Ship translations as `Localization/<localeCode>.csv` files in your mod folder and read them with `ModHelper.GetLocalized(modId, key, args...)`. Resolves the current locale, falls back to English, then to the key itself. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#localization)
- **Custom item sounds.** Item sound fields (`soundUse`, `soundDragStart`, `soundContainerOpen`, ...) now accept mod sound identifiers: `item.soundUse = ModHelper.GetSoundIdentifier("my_mod_id", "gulp");`
- **`ModHook.OnModAssetsLoaded(LoadedMod)`**: fires once per enabled mod when all of its textures, item sprites, and sounds are in memory. Check `mod.Manifest.ID` is yours; it fires for every mod. [Details](ModdingGuide.md#knowing-when-your-assets-are-ready)

**Policy**

- **Decompilation: look, don't copy.** Decompiling the game to study it and find patch targets is officially tolerated; redistributing game code or assets is not. [Full policy](CodeOfConduct.md#respect-other-creators)
